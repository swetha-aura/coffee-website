01 — Origin

Grown slowly,
picked by hand.

Our house lot comes from Finca La Esperanza, a four-hectare farm tucked into the hills above Pitalito. The Caturra trees grow under shade at 1,780 metres, where cool nights stretch the ripening season and pull sugar into every cherry.

Don Elías and his family pick in three passes, selecting only deep-red fruit. The lot is fermented for 72 hours, washed in spring water, then dried slowly on raised beds — a process that gives the cup its fig sweetness and long, tea-like finish.

“We don’t rush the cherry. The coffee already knows what it wants to become.”
Don Elías · Finca La Esperanza

02 — Process

Three hands,
one continuous thread.

Nothing about this cup is accidental. Follow the same bean through the farm, the drum and the brew bar — the line below is the route it takes.

01

Farm

Cherries are picked in three passes, floated, then fermented for 72 hours in small tanks. Raised-bed drying takes eleven days and a lot of patience.

  • Hand-picked
  • 72h ferment
  • Raised bed

02

Roast

A 12-kilogram drum, a short development phase and a profile built for filter and espresso alike. First crack at 9:20; dropped at 11:05, every batch logged by hand.

  • 12kg drum
  • 11:05 drop
  • Omni profile

03

Brew

Rest for 48 hours, then dial in. 18 grams in, 36 out, 28 seconds for espresso; a 1:16 V60 for the slower mornings. Every cup is weighed.

  • 18g / 36g
  • 28s espresso
  • 1:16 V60
